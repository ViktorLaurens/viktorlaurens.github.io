Every controller of chapters 7 and 8, side by side on one clock, on the same "real" pendulum
(with its dry friction, voltage held between samples of $500\ \text{Hz}$, at most $\pm 10$ V).
Each tile shows the controller and its section; its result appears the moment it happens, and,
near the end, where the arm ended up if that is far from its start. The learned agents are
the median seed of their five, by score: a typical agent, not the best one.

The controllers see the true state unless a tile says "encoders": then they see only the two
encoder angles, through the estimators of section 6.6. The trajectories come from
`scripts/make_gallery.py`, the animations from
`animations/gallery_scenes.py`.

## Swinging up

<Figure video="/media/pendulum-lab/gallery_swing_up.mp4" width={100} alt="Nine small Furuta pendulums swing up from rest at the same time, each under another controller: energy and LQR, TVLQR, the hybrid of plan and energy, nonlinear MPC, three reinforcement learning agents and two residual agents. All end upright; the energy controller needs about 4.6 seconds and several swings, the others 1 to 1.6 seconds. Notes appear where the arm ends far away: at 188 degrees for the two controllers that follow the plan, 162 degrees for the agent from scratch, 58 degrees for the residual agent with the large correction">

From rest, at half speed

</Figure>

What to look at:

- **The energy controller (7.3)** pumps for four and a half seconds, swinging the arm through
  more than $300^\circ$; everything else gets up in one to one and a half seconds.
- **TVLQR and the hybrid (7.4)** follow the same optimal plan to the top. The plan ends with the
  arm at $188^\circ$, and that is where they balance.
- **The learned agents (8.1, 8.2, 8.4)** are the fastest, but each in its own way: the agent from
  scratch lets the arm drift to $162^\circ$, the one with the stronger arm weight brings it back,
  the one behind the filter keeps it within $45^\circ$. The two residual agents start from the
  energy controller's swing and cut it short.

## Balancing, and a push

<Figure video="/media/pendulum-lab/gallery_balance.mp4" width={100} alt="Nine small pendulums start 8 degrees from upright and get a push at 2.5 seconds: PID, LQR, LQG on encoders, offset-free LQG with an amplifier offset, linear MPC, nonlinear MPC, LQR behind a barrier, LQR behind the predictive filter, and a reinforcement learning agent. All but one catch the pendulum and recover from the push within 0.1 to 0.35 seconds; under PID the arm keeps turning, three full turns by the end; the LQR behind the barrier drops the pendulum at once while its arm stays within 9 degrees; the learning agent stays within 2 degrees through the push but its arm ends at 127 degrees">

From $8^\circ$ off upright, a push of $1.5\ \text{rad/s}$ on the pendulum at $2.5$ s, at half
speed

</Figure>

What to look at:

- **PID (7.1)** does hold the pendulum up, and recovers from the push fastest. But it never
  sees the arm, which turns away: three full turns in six seconds. That is the failure section
  7.1 is about.
- **LQR, LQG, the offset-free LQG and both MPCs (7.2 to 7.7)** all catch the pendulum and are
  back within $2^\circ$ some $0.2$ to $0.3$ s after the push, the arm close to where it was. The
  LQG does it from the encoders alone; the offset-free one also despite $0.5$ V of amplifier
  offset.
- **The barrier (7.8)** keeps the arm within $15^\circ$, but catching an $8^\circ$ tilt needs
  more arm travel than that, and the barrier only looks at the arm: it lets the pendulum fall.
  **The predictive filter** next to it, with the same limit, plans ahead and catches it. That is
  the difference section 7.8 is about.
- **The learning agent (8.1)** holds the tilt within $2^\circ$ even through the push, the
  firmest of all, and lets the arm wander to $127^\circ$ meanwhile.

## A 50 g payload

<Figure video="/media/pendulum-lab/gallery_payload.mp4" width={100} alt="Six small pendulums with a 50 gram payload at the tip, about twice the pendulum's own mass, start 5 degrees from hanging. The energy controller and the nonlinear MPC with the nominal model never get it up. The nonlinear MPC with the model re-identified after one failed attempt balances at 1.99 seconds, like the one given the exact model. A reinforcement learning agent and a residual agent, both trained on payloads of 0 to 50 grams, balance at 3.1 and 2.8 seconds">

A 50 g payload at the tip, from $5^\circ$ off hanging, at half speed

</Figure>

What to look at:

- **Designed for the pendulum without the payload**, neither the energy controller nor the
  nonlinear MPC gets it up.
- **The nonlinear MPC with the physics re-identified after one failed attempt (8.3)** swings it
  up exactly like the one given the exact model, in $1.99$ s.
- **The agents trained on payloads of 0 to 50 g (8.4)** get it up too, a second later; the one
  on top of the energy controller with the arm near where it started, the one from scratch with
  the arm at $65^\circ$.

## Making them again

```bash
uv run python scripts/make_gallery.py
```

```bash
uv run --group anim python scripts/render_animations.py GallerySwingUp GalleryBalance GalleryPayload
```

The first needs the trained agents and results of
`scripts/evaluate_rl.py` (training rounds 1 and 3, safe and
residual; sections 8.1, 8.2 and 8.4). It runs every controller of a gallery in parallel, in a
minute or two.

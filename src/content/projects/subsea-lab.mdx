## Why under water

The pendulum study ended on an underwater manipulator in the plane and five sentences that
held there: the model is the first thing to fix and the last to trust; the estimate is the
limit; learning helps where it has structure to stand on; a robust filter pays in reach; a
free residual network inside an optimizer diverges. This project takes the same discipline
into three dimensions and into water, where three things change that no pendulum and no
fixed-base arm can ask. The current never stops, so the safe state is not standing still but
a controller holding station with finite thrust. Position is not a fix but a pose relative to
the structure, seen through water of varying turbidity, with an error that must be bounded
before it can be trusted. And the object in the jaws is the thing nobody measured: after
every model has been identified, what is left is its weight, its mass and its drag.

The system is a BlueROV2 Heavy with a four-joint arm, modelled from first principles,
identified by a safe active campaign, and flown on one fixed mission that is never widened:
in a $0.2\ \text{m/s}$ current with gusts, next to a structure with a keep-out zone, approach
from three metres, hold station, reach a handle, grasp an object of unknown mass, buoyancy and
drag, lift it clear and carry it back. Every figure, every number and every negative result
comes from the same code, one script per chapter, with the runs cached so that a chapter can
be regenerated.

## Selected results

All results on this page and in the chapters come from simulation; the plant and the
designer's model differ by construction, and the grasp is a contact in MuJoCo beside the
plant.

Safety has a price in reach, and the price is the uncertainty of the water times its
strength: with the current known the barrier costs nothing at any flow up to
$0.4\ \text{m/s}$; unknown, each $0.1\ \text{m/s}$ of uncertainty costs two centimetres of

A camera pose with a calibrated, turbidity-dependent error bound and a gate brings the tip
from 4.4 cm of the handle to one ([chapter 4](/projects/subsea-lab/04-camera/)); the same
bound used as the safety filter's margin recovers nine centimetres of reach and keeps exactly
the safety the pose promised, which is not the safety the water demands
([chapter 6](/projects/subsea-lab/06-barriers/)).

</Figure>

After identification, what is left to learn is the object, and three numbers by least
squares explain it better off the training flight than a network does; what the physics
structure earns is not accuracy but a certificate, a model that cannot put energy into the
system at a thousand states it was never trained on
([chapter 7](/projects/subsea-lab/07-learning-inside-the-physics/)).

</Figure>

Three numbers re-identified from the raw sensors ten times a second find a new object in
two to four seconds, a buoyant one or one twice the heaviest trained; a latent trained over
24 objects answers nonsense a few newtons past its edge
([chapter 8](/projects/subsea-lab/08-adapting-after-the-grasp/)).

## The storyline: three questions only water asks

**Where is safe when standing still is not an option?** Chapter 1 builds the vehicle and
the arm in three dimensions and a compiled twin of the equations; chapter 5 makes the mission
fly and asks who should move, the vehicle or the arm; chapter 6 builds the barriers in the
structure's frame under a current that never stops, with station keeping as the fallback,
and measures the reach budget; chapter 10 adds the monitors that find out the model is wrong,
with a detection time measured against a time to harm.

**What does the camera buy?** Chapter 2 identifies the vehicle safely, inside a box, in a
current; chapter 3 estimates its state, its heading without a compass, the current and the
force at the jaws, and finds the floor the estimate sets on the tip; chapter 4 replaces the
acoustic fix with a pose relative to the structure, bounded by what the camera can see
through the water.

**What may be learned inside the physics?** Chapter 7 learns the residual after
identification with and without structure and tests the certificate where an optimizer
would; chapter 8 adapts to an unknown object from the raw sensors, physics against structure
against context; chapter 9 sets every controller, learned and not, against each other on the
one mission.

The closing summary, in chapter 10, is the account of what scaled from the pendulum and the
arm and what the water changed.

## How it was made

The report lives in a private repository with the code a committee could read: the physics
in one package, one evaluation script per chapter with its runs cached, figures that only
read those runs, a test suite that includes the chapters' own rules (every figure must exist
with a full-sentence description, every formula must render), and a decisions file that says
why things are the way they are. The site's pages are converted from the report's Markdown
by a script, so that what is read here is what was reviewed there. Two preprints are drawn
from it: safety when the safe state moves (chapters 6 and 10), and what may be learned
inside the physics (chapters 7 and 8).
